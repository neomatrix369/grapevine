# 1. Problem

**Who has the problem?**
> **Decided 19:31 (Mani, kept fallback):** people active in several Slack and Discord communities, newcomers and regulars alike, plus the organisers who run them.

**What do they do today, and what hurts about it?**
> **Decided 19:31 (Mani, kept fallback):**
> - **Knowledge is scattered.** Good answers sit in old threads across many servers and workspaces, so the same questions get asked again.
> - **Experts are invisible.** Nobody can see who the go-to people are for a topic, or why.
> - **Asking is guesswork.** Which channel, which community, whose rules? Posting in the wrong place or cross-posting costs goodwill.
> - **Your own posts get lost.** Once you've asked in three places, you lose track of which got answers.
> - **Nobody sees the whole picture.** There's no sense of how much a community knows, or how that grows.
> - **Organisers fly blind.** Event and hackathon organisers don't know where an announcement is allowed, whom to contact, who could mentor, judge or speak, or which questions keep going unanswered.

**How often does it happen, and what does it cost them?**
> **Decided 19:31 (Mani, kept fallback):** every time someone has a question. It costs time searching across apps, slow or missing answers, repeated questions, and goodwill when a post lands in the wrong place.

**What would "solved" look like?**
> **Decided 19:31 (Mani, kept fallback):** one place that holds the community's knowledge library and its experts, lets you write a question once and send it to the right channels (optionally tagging the right people, within each community's rules), keeps track of your posts and their replies, and shows the knowledge growing as more is read.

**Why now, and why us?**
> **Decided 19:31 (Mani, kept fallback):** communities are multiplying across platforms, and an assistant can now read them (public channels only) and organise them. We live in these communities ourselves.

**Problem statement (one sentence):**
> **Decided 19:31 (Mani):** Community knowledge is scattered across Slack and Discord, so people can't see what's already known or who the experts are, guess where and how to ask, and lose track of their own questions, while organisers can't tell where to announce or who could mentor or judge.
